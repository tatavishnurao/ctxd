# New hire onboarding guide

Fictional sample content for the ctxd dashboard demo.

## Before day one

People Operations sends the offer packet, the start date and the address of the office or the remote equipment shipment. The hiring manager names an onboarding buddy from the same team at least one week before the start date. The buddy is a peer, not a manager, and is the first person to ask about anything that is not written down. IT ships the laptop so that it arrives two working days before the start date. If the laptop has not arrived by then, the new hire emails the IT help desk and the hiring manager.

## Day one checklist

Sign in to the laptop with the temporary password from the welcome email and change it at once. Enroll in single sign-on and set up the authenticator app. Join the company chat, the team channel and the announcements channel. Read and sign the code of conduct and the acceptable use policy. Meet the onboarding buddy for thirty minutes. Meet the hiring manager to agree on the goals for the first month. Do not try to ship code on day one; the day is for access and introductions.

## Accounts and access

Access is granted by role, not by request. The role template for each team lists the systems a new hire receives on day one: source control, the issue tracker, the documentation wiki and the team calendar. Production access is not part of any template. It is granted after the first month, once the new hire has finished the security training and shadowed one on-call shift. Ask for extra access through the access request form, never by asking a colleague to share credentials.

## Equipment

Every employee receives a laptop, a monitor, a keyboard, a mouse and a headset. Remote employees can also claim a home office allowance once per year for a desk or a chair. Keep the receipt; claims follow the expense policy. Report broken equipment to the IT help desk. Do not install unapproved software, and do not disable the disk encryption or the device management agent.

## The first two weeks

Week one is reading: the architecture overview, the team's service catalog, the last three incident reviews and the product roadmap. Week two is a first small change: the buddy picks a starter issue that touches one service and has a clear test. The new hire pairs with the buddy on the change, opens the pull request and follows it through review and deploy.

## Thirty, sixty and ninety day goals

At thirty days the new hire has shipped at least one change to production and can explain how their team's main service handles a request. At sixty days they own a small feature end to end. At ninety days they join the on-call rotation as a secondary and lead one design discussion. The hiring manager reviews progress against these goals at each checkpoint and writes down any changes.

## Training courses

Three courses are mandatory in the first month: security awareness, data privacy and the code of conduct. Each course takes under an hour and ends with a short quiz. Engineers also take the incident response course before they join on-call. Course completion is tracked automatically; the manager receives a reminder when a course is overdue.

## Benefits enrollment

Health, dental and vision cover start on the first day of the month after the start date. The new hire chooses a plan in the benefits portal within thirty days of starting; after that window, changes are only possible at the yearly open enrollment or after a life event such as a birth or a marriage. The retirement plan accepts contributions from the first payroll, and the company match starts after ninety days. People Operations runs a short benefits session for new hires every second Wednesday, and the recording is in the new hires channel for anyone who cannot attend.

## Who to ask

Ask the onboarding buddy about the team and the codebase. Ask the hiring manager about goals, priorities and performance. Ask People Operations about pay, benefits and leave. Ask the IT help desk about laptops, accounts and passwords. When nobody seems to own a question, post it in the new hires channel; someone who joined recently has usually had the same question.
